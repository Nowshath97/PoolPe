01 / The idea

Regular contributions.
One shared pool.

A chit is an arrangement with a defined group, contribution schedule and term. Subscribers pay into the pool, and a subscriber becomes entitled to a payout each cycle through the method set out in the agreement.

Auctions are one common method. Other permitted methods can include a draw or tender, depending on the agreement and applicable rules.

A payout is not free money. The recipient remains responsible for future subscriptions under the agreement.
1

Agree on the commitment

Review the term, subscription, selection method, fees and payout conditions before joining.

2

Contribute each cycle

Members make the scheduled payments. Together, these form that cycle’s gross chit amount.

3

Determine the recipient

In an auction, eligible subscribers offer a discount they are willing to accept. Selection follows the group’s agreed auction rules.

4

Calculate and record the payout

The discount reduces the prize amount. Fees and the distributable share are accounted for under the agreement.

5

Continue through the full term

The cycle repeats for the remaining subscribers. Members who have received a payout continue their scheduled contributions.

02 / Know the language

Six terms that make it click.

Subscription
The periodic contribution agreed for a ticket in the chit.
Gross chit amount
The total subscriptions for one instalment, before discount or other deductions.
Foreman
The organiser responsible for conducting the chit and administering its agreement.
Discount
The portion the selected subscriber agrees to forgo from the gross chit amount.
Prize amount
The gross chit amount less the discount; payment is subject to the agreement’s conditions.
Share of discount
The distributable portion of the discount allocated to subscribers as agreed, often called a dividend.
03 / Follow the numbers

One cycle,
without the guesswork.

Imagine 20 members, each holding one ticket and contributing ₹5,000 a month, over 20 monthly cycles. The gross pool for a cycle is ₹1,00,000.

For this example, the selected member accepts a ₹20,000 discount. Assume an agreed organiser fee of ₹5,000 is taken from that discount, with the balance shared equally across all 20 tickets.

The ₹750 share is an allocation of this cycle’s discount, not guaranteed interest. Whether it is paid out or adjusted against a subscription depends on the agreement.

04 / Make an informed decision

Clarity first.
Commitment second.

Chit arrangements involve payment and organiser risks. An app or a well-presented website does not establish that a chit is registered or suitable for you.

  • Verify the organiser and the chit’s registration with the relevant state authority.
  • Read the written agreement, including fees, default rules, exit terms and dispute procedures.
  • Ask about payout timing, documents, security or guarantor requirements.
  • Budget for every instalment, including those after your payout.
  • Keep receipts and reconcile your payment records with the organiser.

05 / Common questions

A few things worth knowing.

Does every member receive the same amount?

Not necessarily. In an auction chit, the discount may vary each cycle, so prize amounts can differ. Your overall outcome also depends on fees and the shares of discount allocated over the term.

Can I stop paying once I receive my prize?

No. You remain liable for the remaining subscriptions under your agreement. Check any security requirements and consequences of missing a payment before participating.

Are chit funds regulated by the RBI?

Chit fund companies are regulated by the respective state governments, rather than by the RBI as banks. Verify a chit’s registration with the relevant Registrar of Chits. A PoolPay account does not verify or replace registration.

Is a payout or return guaranteed?

This guide does not promise either. Outcomes depend on the agreement, auction results, member payments and the organiser’s performance. Delays or defaults can affect participants.

Is this example the same as my PoolPay group’s calculation?

It may not be. This example assumes the organiser fee comes from the discount and the balance is shared across every ticket. Check your actual agreement and the calculation recorded by your manager; do not use this illustration as your payment schedule.

Do I need an account to read this guide?

No. You can read and share this page without logging in. An account is only needed for your private dashboard and group records.
